### 2.2 Economic assumptions and scenario reliability

What the results are most sensitive to, in order of evidence:

1. **Lease capital and oil price.** Oil at $45 for five years or redevelopment capital +30% removes every lease from book D (`results/v8/whatifs.csv`). The lease side is a bet on an assumed cost. Already reported; keep it prominent.
2. **ERCOT volatility and mean reversion.** Half the volatility drops the gas requests and the agreement-backed solar request; book D held under that world expects $1.30B instead of $1.92B, with a worst-5% loss of $1.09B against $0.55B for the re-solved book (`whatifs.csv`). The gas request draws 68% of its value from its best 5% of scenarios [R4].
3. **Completion probabilities.** The lookup and the model disagree on level; the 2020 backtest ratio (0.614) corrects the model, but the two backtests disagree.
4. **Merchant vs contracted.** Contracted revenue turns six of twenty requests positive (notebook 07 part B).

*Monte Carlo vs structural uncertainty.* The 2,000 + 2,000 scenarios measure uncertainty within one generator. Structural uncertainty — which generator — is not measured. The minimal test, with existing code: score books A–D (and the challenger's alternatives) under three alternative generators, each a few lines in `src/scenarios.py`: (a) no mean reversion target above 2024 (revert to 2024 levels); (b) the block bootstrap without the February 2021 block; (c) doubled shock size. If D − A keeps its sign in all three, the ranking is structurally robust; if not, the report says which assumption carries it.

### 2.3 Capital feasibility

[R3] shows the reserve is a mean, not a plan. Three options, in order of cost:

1. **Report it** (no new model): the distribution of draws beyond the reserve, and the liquidity line needed at the 90th or 95th percentile. Cost: one notebook cell.
2. **Funding-or-forfeit recourse** (moderate): in each scenario, requests that reach their agreement are funded in order of value until a stated liquidity line runs out; the rest forfeit their deposits. The book's value is then scored with the recourse. This is a two-stage evaluation, not a two-stage optimization, and it uses the existing `op_year` draws.
3. **Chance constraint in the MILP** (higher): P(draws ≤ reserve + line) ≥ 1 − ε, linearised with scenario indicators. Justified only if option 2 shows the recourse changes the ranking of books.

Recommendation: do 1 and 2; do 3 only if 2 changes the answer.

### 2.4 Financial interpretation

- **$293M D − A (committee grid).** Robust in sign to re-selection ([R2]: $176M–$485M). It is post-registration: the committee grid is post-hoc, so the "registered metric" applied to it is a registered *formula* on an unregistered grid. Label it that way.
- **λ.** Scale-dependent (V8). Present the frontier by policy and let the committee pick a limit.
- **Registered vs committee grid.** The two grids give different books; the committee grid is the decision case and the registered grid the record. Keep both; say once which one the memo uses.
- **−$13M revision.** The value of one heuristic revision policy under the cap, not of flexibility (V6). An optimal policy that may also "hold" cannot be worth less than static in expectation in sample; a negative held-out value is estimation error in the continuation regressions plus the cap. Say so.
- **Value of information.** A ceiling, risk-neutral (V7). The six "defer" statuses compare a risk-neutral EVSI with a deposit; recompute at λ = 1 or label.
- **ERCOT scarcity.** The gas request's value is mostly scarcity outcomes [R4]; any statement about it must carry that.

### 2.5 Predictive vs decision value

Why the better-ranking model does not give a better book:

1. **The decision is concentrated.** The book's value turns on a few large requests (the gas request alone: $445M held-out mean). Ranking accuracy averaged over 1,649 requests says little about the five that matter.
2. **Tail and selection noise.** The 100 scenarios of a CVaR95 estimate move by about $0.2B between two draws of the same generator, the same order as the differences between books; D, chosen on those scenarios, loses $109M of expected value out of sample, F $3M [R1].
3. **Scale.** F's book is smaller; the absolute CVaR rewards that.

Decision-focused evaluation to add (no new predictive algorithm): (i) *value-weighted* ranking metrics — AUC or Brier weighted by each request's NPV-if-built — so forecast quality is measured where money is; (ii) the ML increment scored under re-selection ([R2]-style) and per budget dollar; (iii) a "forecast-perturbation" test: shift the model's probabilities for the five largest requests within their backtest error and record whether the book changes.

## Deliverable 2 — The Active Prescriptive AI Decision Advisor

### 2.1 What changes, and why it is scientifically meaningful

Today the advisor is a translator and explainer: words → validated parameters → computed fact sheet → checked explanation. Its investment increment is zero by construction, and the report says so. That is correct, and it bounds what an LLM can claim.

The extension tests a different hypothesis: **an LLM that challenges both the human and the optimizer, using validated tools, improves the specification and scrutiny of the decision**. It never searches for a better book under the same objective, scenarios and constraints — the MILP is optimal there, and an LLM cannot beat it. Every improvement it claims must come from an identifiable mechanism:

| Mechanism | What the advisor does | Tool that makes it checkable |
|---|---|---|
| Missing constraint | Notices that the plan ignores capital calls | `capital_calls(book)` [new] |
| Fragile assumption | Notices that a member's value rests on rare outcomes or one scenario model | `tail_dependence(candidate)`, `model_set(book)` [new] |
| Objective mismatch | Compares the committee's stated preferences (in words) with the λ the book was solved at | `whatif` with `cvar_limit_musd`, P(loss) limit [new parameter] |
| Selection noise | Separates stable members from swing members | `selection_stability(book)` [new] |
| Information | Recommends waiting when the signal is worth more than the deposit | `voi` [exists] |
| Human inconsistency | Shows where the committee's own statement conflicts with its own stated rule | motivated-reasoning check [exists], `compare(book_a, book_b)` [exists as `paired_scenario_bootstrap`] |

### 2.2 Tools (typed, deterministic, all numbers computed)

Existing: `whatif.Engine` (reprice and re-solve), `whatif.explain` (opportunity cost of forcing a member in or out), `voi.*`, `dynamic.staged_policy`, `optimizer.paired_scenario_bootstrap`, `ledger.record`, `advisor_tools.verify_attributed`.

New, each a small function in `src/` with a test:

1. `tail_dependence(candidate)` — P(loss), share of total value from the best 5% of scenarios, value conditional on ERCOT and oil price-level deciles.
2. `risk_contributions(book)` — each member's mean NPV in the book's worst 5% (partly exists as `cvar_share` in `project_impact.csv`).
3. `capital_calls(book, line)` — draws beyond the reserve, P(shortfall), the line needed at a stated percentile, value under funding-or-forfeit.
4. `model_set(book)` — the book scored under the three alternative generators of §2.2 and the lookup's completion curves.
5. `selection_stability(book)` — how often each member is selected across resampled primary sets.
6. `preference_check(book, stated)` — stated preferences (a P(loss) cap, a tail cap, an exclusion, a minimum utilization) as parameters; returns which the book violates.

### 2.3 Output contract

A *challenge memo*, JSON, checked before it is shown:

- `vulnerabilities`: at most three, ranked by dollar consequence. Each carries `claim`, `kind` ∈ {computed fact, assumption, inference, hypothesis to test}, `tool_call_id`, and its numbers. A computed fact without a tool call is rejected; a hypothesis must name the tool call that would test it.
- `alternatives`: books the tools computed, with the paired comparison and interval.
- `level`: explain / challenge / guide. "Guide" requires that one alternative beats the base on the committee's stated objective with an interval clear of zero, or that the base violates a stated constraint.
- `what_would_change_it` and `evidence_insufficient` (an allowed answer).
- `verify_attributed` must pass on every number.

### 2.4 Authority and the human

Level 1 explains, Level 2 challenges, Level 3 guides. The named authorizer decides at every level. The ledger (`src/ledger.py`) already refuses an override without five answers and the opposing case; add one field: *material contrary evidence acknowledged*, required when the chosen book is dominated, on the committee's stated objective, by an alternative the advisor computed. The human may still choose it; the ledger records that they did so knowingly.

Bias detection is restricted to observable inconsistencies: a statement that cites a sunk cost; a preference stated as a rule and then broken by the chosen book; a judgment of one sector on the mean and another on the tail. Disagreeing with the model is not evidence of bias.

### 2.5 Example 1 — the ERCOT gas request (your example, with the numbers checked)

*Optimizer.* Book D (committee grid, λ = 1) holds ERCOT|25INR0008, 1,350 MW of gas four years in the queue. On the held-out scenarios it expects $445M and loses money in 79% of them [R4]; on the primary scenarios $499M and 78% (`explanations.csv`, notebook 08).

*Challenger, Level 2.* Three questions, each answered by a tool:

1. *Does the committee accept a 79% chance of loss on its largest power position?* `tail_dependence`: 68% of the request's total value comes from its best 5% of scenarios [R4] — it is an option on scarcity, not an asset.
2. *Is it worth its place?* `explain`: forcing it out lowers book D's objective by only $24.5M — the book gives up $433M of expected value and its worst-5% loss shrinks by $408M (`results/v8/explanations.csv`). The request sits at indifference: at a tail weight slightly above 1 it leaves.
3. *Does it survive a different price model?* `whatif(ercot_volatility=0.5)`: the re-solved book drops both gas requests; book D held in that world expects $1.30B with a worst-5% loss of $1.09B, against $1.29B and $0.55B for the re-solved book (`whatifs.csv`). `model_set` would add the alternative generators.

*Challenger, Level 3 (only if the evidence supports it).* If the committee states its preference as "no more than a one-in-ten chance of loss on any position above $500M of capital", the gas request violates it, and the advisor recommends the re-solved book without it, stating the $433M of expected value given up and the $408M of tail removed. If the committee states λ = 1 and nothing else, the advisor says the evidence does not favour either book — the difference is $24.5M on its own objective — and shows the trade-off.

*Human.* The committee receives the recommendation, the opposing case and the dollar consequence of each choice, and authorizes. *Measured outcome:* the held-out and model-set scores of both books, recorded in the ledger with the decision.

This is the story the report can tell: Prediction → Uncertainty → Prescription → AI Challenge → Human Decision → Measured Outcome.

### 2.6 Example 2 — the missing constraint (capital calls)

*Optimizer.* Book D charges each request its deposits plus a reserve equal to its expected construction draw; the budget binds at 99.8%.

*Challenger.* `capital_calls(book D)`: the reserve ($2.00B) is exceeded in 45.5% of held-out scenarios, by $1.42B on average when it is, and by $3.31B at the 95th percentile [R3]. The advisor flags a missing constraint — not a better book under the old constraints — and asks the committee for its liquidity line. With a stated line, `capital_calls` scores book D under funding-or-forfeit and the engine re-solves with the requirement.

*Why it is a legitimate improvement.* The objective and constraints change because the committee supplied information the model lacked. The optimizer then finds the new optimum; the advisor's contribution is the specification, and the experiment credits it only with that.

## Deliverable 3 — Experimental design

*Attribution principle.* The LLM is credited only with what a deterministic procedure using the same tools does not do. Every LLM condition therefore has a **checklist control**: the same tools run in a fixed order with fixed thresholds, no language model. If the checklist matches the LLM, the LLM's contribution is convenience, not decision value, and the report says so.

*Processes* (your table, mapped to what can be run):

| System | Here | Runs on current data? |
|---|---|---|
| A conventional rules | Books A, B | yes (exists) |
| B deterministic NPV | Book C | yes (exists) |
| C risk-aware optimizer | Book D | yes (exists) |
| D optimizer + explanatory LLM | v8 advisor (Level 1) | yes (exists) |
| E optimizer + active challenger | new (Level 2–3) + checklist control | yes, with simulated committee statements |
| F human + active challenger | needs participants | no — pilot only, labelled |

### Experiments that need only current data and code

**X1 Seeded-flaw benchmark** (detection). 48 decision cases written and keyed *before* any run: a committee statement and a proposed book, with zero or one flaw from a fixed list — cap violation, capital-call infeasibility, tail reliance on one position, sunk-cost argument, mean-vs-median double standard, a preference stated and then broken, a stale or out-of-scope claim (e.g., interest rates). One third are clean. Outcomes: recall and precision of flaw detection; false-challenge rate on clean cases; unsupported claims; factual error rate. Comparators: checklist control; Level-1 advisor. *Hypothesis:* the challenger's recall exceeds the checklist's on the flaws that need reading the statement (sunk cost, double standard, broken preference) with a false-challenge rate ≤ 15%. *Falsified* if recall − checklist recall has a paired interval including zero, or false challenges exceed 15%. McNemar test on paired detections.

**X2 Misspecified worlds** (decision value under model uncertainty). The optimizer chooses in the base model; the "truth" is each of four worlds: the three alternative generators of §2.2 and the lookup's completion curves. The challenger may recommend, from a closed menu, book D, D without its most tail-dependent member, the max-min-regret book across the model set, or a capital-constrained D. Outcome: held-out regret in each world against the best book in that world. Comparators: D alone; the checklist (always pick max-min regret when `model_set` shows a sign change). *Falsified* if the challenger's regret is not below D's, or not below the checklist's, with paired intervals clear of zero. This is the only experiment that can show investment value, and the credit is explicitly for responding to model uncertainty.

**X3 Preference translation** (alignment). 40 committee preference statements in words with keyed parameters, including 8 out of scope. Outcomes: exact-match accuracy, correct refusal, and whether the resulting book satisfies the stated preferences. Comparator: a structured form (accuracy 1 by construction). The LLM's value here is convenience; the experiment measures its error rate, which bounds the harm.

**X4 Over-reliance and error injection.** In 10 cases the advisor's fact sheet carries one deliberately wrong number; measure whether `verify_attributed` blocks it (should be 10 of 10) and whether the challenge memo still recommends the dominated book. Measures a failure mode, not a benefit.

**X5 Gym with the challenger.** In the 14 effective situations, instinct proposes and the challenger may object using the gym's tools. Score instinct + challenge against instinct and the rule. Power is low (n = 14); report as descriptive.

*Controls and reporting.* Cases, keys and thresholds committed before the first run, as new registered lines (C25 onward), with the model id, temperature and prompt hashes; every call recorded and replayed; costs (tokens, seconds) per case. Paired bootstrap intervals over cases; separate investment value (X2), model-specification value (X1, X2 menu choice) and user-experience value (X3, time).

### What needs human participants

A within-subject pilot with 6–10 peers deciding two cases with and without the challenger: agreement with their own stated preferences, time, and reliance (do they accept a planted wrong recommendation?). It needs consent and a protocol the course must allow. If it cannot run before 14 December, it is described as future work and nothing in the report implies a human benefit.

## Deliverable 6 — Final recommendation

1. **Is it a strong MADS capstone now?** Yes, with one claim that must be repaired. The registered record, the audit, the equal-constraint benchmarking and the honest zero for the AI's investment increment place it well above the usual standard. But the report says the decision uses only what was knowable on 1 January 2025, and three inputs break that (V2). The other weaknesses — capital feasibility, selection noise, structural uncertainty — are fixable with existing code.
2. **Three most defensible contributions.** (a) A prescriptive pipeline evaluated against conventional rules under equal constraints on held-out scenarios, with its failures kept. (b) Evidence that better prediction need not give a better decision, with the mechanism identified (concentration, tail noise, scale). (c) An AI layer that cannot invent numbers and whose contribution is measured separately from the optimizer's.
3. **Corrections needed before submission.** V2 first (the 2027 credit cliff is post-date law; agreement dates and lease months from later releases), because every number downstream moves; then V1 (capital calls), V3/V4 (selection noise, ML increment restated), V6–V8 (framing). V5 if time allows, as a small model set. After the V2 re-run, every headline number — including the $293M — must be re-read from `results/`; none of this review's figures should be carried into the report unchanged.
4. **Is active LLM-guided decision intelligence a meaningful extension?** Yes, if it is framed as a test of specification and scrutiny, not of selection, and if it carries a non-LLM checklist control. Without that control it would credit the LLM with the tools' work.
5. **How to measure it without crediting the optimizer.** Hold the optimizer fixed; credit the LLM only for (i) flaws found beyond the checklist (X1), (ii) regret reduced in misspecified worlds beyond a deterministic robust rule (X2). Report zero where it is zero.
6. **Best achievable scope by 14 December.** Phase 1 in full; Phase 2 with tools 1–3 and 6 (`tail_dependence`, `risk_contributions`, `capital_calls`, `preference_check`); X1 and X3 in full, X2 with two alternative worlds; the restructured report.
7. **Defer to future work.** The human-participant study; chance-constrained or fully two-stage optimization (unless funding-or-forfeit changes the answer); a full model set beyond three generators; real redevelopment costs; live committee use of the decision page.

- **Report:** an executive report; core up to about 4,500 words; findings open with their conclusion; methods, the record and the audit in appendices.
