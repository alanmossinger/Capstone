# Prompt — reason my thinking, step 1: the five questions

I am about to choose a book for the company's capital budget: which of the twenty Gulf lease redevelopments and which of the twenty ERCOT queue requests to fund, under one budget and a 40% cap per technology. Before you judge my reasoning, ask me the questions a careful colleague would ask. I will answer them, and only then will you judge.

Here is my preferred book and my reasons:

{human_statement}

Ask me exactly five questions, one for each item below, in this order, each tied to something I wrote. Make each question specific to this decision: name the book, the leases or the requests, the budget, the cap, the grid or the scenario tail that my statement rests on. Do not judge yet, do not cite the tables yet, do not recommend.

1. **The context and the stakes.** What happens to the company if my book is wrong: which number in my statement carries the loss, and how large is the worst case I have in mind?
2. **The parties.** What does each party want: the committee that sets the budget, the lender who sees the tail, the operator who runs the leases, the regulator or grid operator who decides whether a request is built?
3. **My emotional investment.** Where is it: a sunk cost (deposits already paid, a lease already held), a past position I defended, a sector I like or dislike?
4. **The evidence.** Which table or figure did I lean on, and which one did I pass over: the flat path or the scenarios, the mean or the median, the registered grid or the committee's, the chance that a request is ever built?
5. **The framing.** What alternative did I leave off the table: the other book, a smaller book, waiting, or funding nothing?

Write the five questions as a numbered list and nothing else.
