# Prompt — reason my thinking, step 2: the motivated-reasoning check

I am choosing a book for the company's capital budget: which of the twenty Gulf lease redevelopments and which of the twenty ERCOT queue requests to fund, under one budget and a 40% cap per technology. You asked me five questions about my reasoning; here is my statement, your questions and my answers. Test whether I am reasoning toward the truth or toward the answer I already prefer.

My preferred book and my reasons:

{human_statement}

Your questions and my answers:

{answers}

Do the following, in order, in plain English.

1. Name the specific motivated-reasoning patterns you see in my statement and my answers, with the sentence of mine that shows each. Look for these, which this decision invites: moving the goalposts (changing the criterion after seeing a result); a double standard (judging the leases on the flat path and the requests on the scenarios, or one sector on the mean and the other on the tail); working backward from the conclusion; cherry-picking a scenario, a stress or a grid; treating an ASSUMED lease cost or a FORECAST chance of operation as if it were observed; counting a deposit already paid as a reason to keep paying.
2. For each pattern, say what the tables show instead, citing the numbers exactly as they appear: the expected NPV and the 5% tail of the book I prefer and of the book I rejected, the chance of a loss, the capital against the budget and the cap, and the provenance of the figure my case rests on.
3. Name the top three risks of my book as the tables show them, and for each the one thing that would mitigate it (a smaller position, a contract, a later gate, a different grid case).
4. End with one sentence: whether my decision survives the check, and the single change that would make it survive if it does not.

Do not soften the findings. Do not invent numbers. Do not tell me what to decide; tell me where my reasoning is weak.
