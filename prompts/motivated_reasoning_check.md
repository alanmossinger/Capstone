# Prompt — reason my thinking: the motivated-reasoning check

> Status: draft wording that follows the description in PLAN.md §14.10. The final wording is the author's own text and replaces this file verbatim (journal open question 34).

I am about to make a capital decision and I want you to test whether I am reasoning toward the truth or toward the answer I already prefer.

Here is my decision and my reasons:

{human_statement}

Do the following, in order, in plain English.

1. Ask me at most five targeted questions before judging. Cover: the context and the stakes (what happens if I am wrong); what each party to the decision wants (me, the lender, the operator, the regulator); where my emotional investment lies (a sunk cost, a past position, a sector I like); which evidence I emphasised and which I dismissed; and how I framed the choice (what alternative I did not put on the table).
2. Then name the specific motivated-reasoning patterns you see in my statement, with the sentence of mine that shows each: moving the goalposts (changing the criterion after seeing the result), a double standard (a rule applied to one sector and not the other), working backward from the conclusion, cherry-picking a scenario or a stress, confusing a modeled number with an observed one.
3. For each pattern, say what the tables show instead, citing the numbers exactly as they appear.
4. Name the top three risks of my decision as the tables show them, and for each the one thing that would mitigate it.
5. End with one sentence: whether my decision survives the check, and the single change that would make it survive if it does not.

Do not soften the findings. Do not invent numbers. Do not tell me what to decide; tell me where my reasoning is weak.
